Quick Start

Using ShootingScanner

  • Select your discipline and make sure the target symbol and the distance match your card.
  • Create a new session with the '+ New' button at the top.
  • Photograph your cards so that the shot holes look white.
  • ShootingScanner works out the score and the group statistics for you.

Scanning Tips

Take a photo of the card showing the whole black aiming mark and making the shot holes look bright. Try laying the card on a white sheet of paper or holding it against a bright laptop screen as a bright background. Good cards help: beige paper gives a strong contrast and pellets cut clean, round holes, easy to find.

Adjusting the sights

When the centre of your group is not in the middle of the target, you can then use the windage and elevation figures to adjust your sights and bring the group back into the centre.

Accuracy

Hole positions are usually found to within a few tenths of a millimetre on a sharp photo. You can always refine the position of a hole yourself as well: tap a hit to select it, drag it into place and use the magnifier that appears to line it up with the hole's edge.

Score analysis

Every shot receives a ring score and a decimal score following the outward gauging rule, and inner tens are counted as X. For each card and for the whole session you also see the average hit, windage, elevation, mean radius and maximum spread in millimetres, while the colour heat map shows you where all your hits cluster, for a single session or for the last 7, 30 or 90 days.

Troubleshooting

The aiming mark has not been found

Check that the whole black aiming mark is in the photo and not cut off by the edge.

Rings do not match the card

Check that the session uses the same target type as your card. Then open the target, choose 'Adjust rings' and drag the picture until the dashed circle sits on the edge of the black, using the size buttons if needed. The ring lines and the pattern must be clearly visible.

Shot holes have not been found

Check the number of shots per target in the session details. ShootingScanner looks for spots that are brighter than the paper around them and the size of a hole. If some holes are missed, open 'Scan again' and raise the sensitivity, or retake the photo with more contrast to the white background.

'Scan again' also lets you say what the holes look like in your photo:

  • Lighter than the paper – the usual case, with something white or light behind the card.
  • Darker than the paper – a dark backing; holes inside the black aiming mark cannot be seen this way.
  • Dark on light rings, light on black – a grey or mid-tone backing, where holes in rings 1–6 look darker than the paper and holes in the black (rings 7–10) look lighter than the black.
  • A colour (green, yellow, black, red or blue) – a coloured backing; the holes are found by their colour on both the paper and the black.

Low accuracy

If some holes were placed in the wrong spot, try a different light or step back a little - a photo taken from a bit further away often has fewer shadows and less lens distortion, so the result is better. Strong light shining through the shot holes makes them look bigger than they really are and lowers the accuracy.

Your saved data

Your sessions and photos are kept in this browser on this device. Use 'Save backup' on the sessions screen now and then, and 'Restore backup' to bring your sessions over to another device or other browser when you switch.
