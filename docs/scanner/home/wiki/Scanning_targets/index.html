Scanning Targets

In order to scan a target you just take a photo of each card and check every hit is in the right place. ShootingScanner does its best with any photo, but a few simple habits make the results much more accurate and more consistent from one session to the next one.

Choosing Quality Cards

Use good quality cards, like those approved for ISSF matches. They help in several ways:

  • Correct pattern - the rings are printed to the official sizes, so the scale ShootingScanner measures from the black aiming mark will match every ring on the card; cards marked as approved for official competition are the safest choice
  • Paper colour - the scanner is tuned for beige card, where light holes stand out well against both the paper and the black, and holes need a different colour than the card itself
  • Clean holes - heavy competition card is made from dense pulp, so pellets and bullets punch neat round holes, simple to score
  • No shine - matte black print does not cause reflections

Choosing the background

Each hole must look lighter than the paper around it. Put something bright behind the card when you take the photo, so that in the picture the shot holes show up as white or light blue. For example you can use:

  • Tablet screen – a tablet works well for small cards: open a plain white page, turn the brightness down a little and lay the card on top of the screen. Then simply take the photo with your phone camera.
  • Laptop screen – open a blank white page on your laptop and rest the card against the screen; then turn the brightness down a bit
  • White sheet of paper - in good daylight, especially when all the shots are inside the black aiming mark, a plain white or light blue sheet of paper under the card is usually all you need.
  • Wall or window - you can also hold a large card against a window or bright wall.

Taking the photo

When you take the photo, make sure that:

  • The lens is clean – wipe it with a clean, soft cloth to remove any dust, grease and old fingerprints
  • The whole aiming mark is in view - frame the photo so that the black aiming mark of the card fills between 20 and 100% of the picture
  • Ring lines are visible – ShootingScanner measures the black aiming mark to set the scale of all of the rings
  • You shoot straight on - hold the camera square to the card; ShootingScanner copes with a small angle, but it is always best to take the photo from straight above, within about 15 degrees of square
  • The camera is still – a blurry photo will never give you accurate results

Editing the scan

ShootingScanner should find the target and the holes on its own, but if needed you can always edit shot location using the magnifier that appears on the target screen.

Keep in mind that a phone camera was never designed to be a scoring machine. To get accurate and consistent results, take a little time to learn what makes a good photo of your target cards.
